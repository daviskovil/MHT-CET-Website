If you are preparing for the MHT CET 2026 question paper, the single highest-leverage thing you can do is stop treating every chapter equally. Some chapters show up in almost every shift; others appear once every few years. Knowing the difference changes how you spend your remaining study hours, and it is the difference between a revision plan that feels busy and one that actually moves your score. Most students already sense, informally, that certain chapters matter more, but very few have ever seen the actual numbers behind that intuition, which is exactly the gap this guide is meant to close.

This guide walks through the complete chapter weightage picture for Physics, Chemistry, and Maths, explains how to turn that data into an actual study plan, covers the mistakes students make when they first discover weightage data, and answers the questions we hear most often from MHT CET aspirants and their parents.

## Why Chapter Weightage Matters

MHT CET draws from 18 shifts a year across three subjects, and the distribution of questions across chapters is not random. Analysing five years of past papers shows a handful of chapters accounting for a disproportionate share of questions in every subject, year after year. A student who spends equal hours on every chapter in the syllabus is, in effect, spending too much time on low-yield topics and too little on the chapters most likely to decide their final percentile.

This is not a new idea. Every serious MHT CET aspirant has heard some version of "focus on important chapters." What is usually missing is the actual data: which chapters, how much more likely are they to appear, and what does that mean for a six-week or twelve-week revision schedule. That is what this guide covers, chapter by chapter, subject by subject.

### How We Calculated This

We tagged every question across MHTCET Simu's library of 16,000+ past year questions by chapter, then counted frequency per subject across the last five years of MHT CET shifts. This is the same tagging system that powers the chapter-accuracy analytics inside the simulator, so the weightage data you see in this article is the same data your practice reports are built on.

A few notes on methodology, because weightage claims are only useful if you trust where they came from. First, we counted at the chapter level, not the sub-topic level, since MHT CET rarely asks more than one or two questions from a single narrow sub-topic in any one shift. Second, we weighted every shift equally rather than only looking at the most recent year, since a single year can be an outlier. Third, where a question could reasonably be tagged under two chapters (common in coordinate geometry and in physical chemistry), we tagged it under the chapter the exam's own syllabus document lists it against.

### What "Weightage" Actually Means for Your Prep

Weightage is not a guarantee. A chapter with high historical weightage can still produce zero questions in a specific shift, and a low-weightage chapter can occasionally spike. What weightage gives you is a probability-weighted view of where your study hours produce the best expected return, averaged across the realistic range of shifts you might get. Over a full paper, and especially over the multiple attempts most students make while preparing, that average is what determines your score. Treat the numbers in this article as a prioritisation tool, not as a shortcut that lets you skip chapters entirely. We will come back to that distinction later in the Common Mistakes section, because it is the single most common way students misuse weightage data.

## Physics: Complete Chapter-by-Chapter Weightage

Physics is where weightage skew is most pronounced. A small cluster of mechanics chapters, combined with electricity and magnetism, regularly accounts for close to half of the Physics section in any given shift.

### The Mechanics Cluster

Rotational Dynamics, Circular Motion, and Gravitation consistently account for the largest share of Physics questions across every year we analysed. These three chapters share a common thread: they lean heavily on a small set of standard formulas applied to slightly varied setups, which makes them attractive for question-setters who want problems that are rigorous but not ambiguous.

Rotational Dynamics alone tends to produce numerical-heavy questions built around moment of inertia, angular momentum conservation, and rolling motion. Students who are comfortable with the standard derivations, but who have not practised enough varied numericals, are the ones who lose marks here, not because the concept is unfamiliar but because the specific combination in the question is new. Circular Motion questions are usually shorter and more direct, often testing banking of roads, conical pendulum setups, or vertical circular motion, and reward students who have memorised the handful of standard result formulas rather than re-deriving them under time pressure. Gravitation rounds out the cluster with questions on orbital velocity, escape velocity, and Kepler's laws, and it is one of the more forgiving chapters in the sense that the question patterns repeat closely from year to year.

The most efficient way to practise this cluster is to work through a chapter-mode set for each of the three chapters separately before mixing them, since a common failure mode is confusing which standard formula applies to which setup once fatigue sets in during a full paper. Once you can solve isolated chapter sets accurately, a multi-chapter mix across all three mechanics chapters together is the right final step, because it forces you to identify the correct approach from the question text alone, which is exactly what the real exam demands.

### The Electricity and Magnetism Cluster

Electrostatics and Current Electricity follow mechanics closely in frequency, especially in shifts held later in the exam window. Electrostatics questions cluster around electric field and potential due to standard charge configurations, capacitor combinations, and Gauss's law applications. Current Electricity leans on Kirchhoff's laws, Wheatstone bridge variants, and combination-of-resistors problems that are computationally straightforward once the circuit is correctly drawn out, which is itself the main source of error under time pressure.

Magnetic Effects of Current and Electromagnetic Induction appear less frequently than the two chapters above but still often enough that they cannot be treated as optional, particularly for students aiming for the top percentile bands where every mark matters.

Because circuit-drawing errors are the main source of lost marks in this cluster, practise redrawing complex circuits into their simplified equivalent form as a standalone drill before attempting full numericals. A few sessions dedicated purely to circuit simplification tend to produce a bigger accuracy jump in Current Electricity than the same time spent on additional formula revision.

### The Modern Physics and Optics Cluster

Modern Physics, covering photoelectric effect, atomic structure, and nuclear physics, produces a steady stream of shorter, more conceptual questions that are highly learnable in a short amount of time relative to the marks they carry, which makes this cluster one of the best return-on-effort areas in the entire Physics syllabus for students revising in the final weeks. Optics, split between ray optics and wave optics, tends to test standard lens and mirror formula applications alongside interference and diffraction pattern questions, and rewards careful sign convention habits more than raw formula memorisation.

If your revision window is short, this cluster is the best place to start rather than the last chapters you get to. The concept-to-marks ratio is higher here than almost anywhere else in Physics, so a focused two or three day pass through Modern Physics and Optics, followed by a chapter-mode test to confirm retention, is one of the highest-leverage moves available in the final weeks before the exam.

## Chemistry: Complete Chapter-by-Chapter Weightage

Chemistry weightage is more evenly spread across its three broad divisions than Physics, but within each division a small number of chapters still dominate.

### The Physical Chemistry Cluster

Chemical Kinetics and Electrochemistry are the two heaviest-weighted chapters in Physical Chemistry. Chemical Kinetics questions typically test rate law determination, order of reaction from experimental data, and the Arrhenius equation, and are some of the most formula-driven, high-accuracy-potential questions in the entire Chemistry section once the underlying logic is understood. Electrochemistry questions built around the Nernst equation, cell EMF calculations, and conductivity are similarly formulaic and, because of that, tend to be among the highest-scoring questions on the paper for students who have practised enough numericals to move quickly.

Solutions, covering colligative properties and Raoult's law, and Thermodynamics, covering enthalpy and entropy calculations, round out the cluster with moderate but consistent weightage.

Physical Chemistry as a cluster rewards students who build a single reference sheet of every formula used across Kinetics, Electrochemistry, Solutions, and Thermodynamics, since the four chapters share enough numerical structure that cross-chapter confusion, rather than genuine lack of knowledge, is the most common source of errors. Reviewing that sheet immediately before a chapter-mode test, rather than trying to recall formulas cold, is a simple habit that measurably improves accuracy in this cluster.

### The Inorganic Chemistry Cluster

p-Block Elements is the single heaviest-weighted chapter in Inorganic Chemistry, with questions spanning group trends, specific compound properties, and reaction-based questions across groups 14 through 18. d and f Block Elements and Coordination Compounds follow closely, with recurring questions on transition metal properties, colour and magnetic behaviour, and coordination compound nomenclature and isomerism. These three chapters share a common preparation challenge: the content is fact-dense rather than formula-driven, which means spaced repetition and active recall matter more here than in the numerical-heavy chapters elsewhere in the syllabus.

Because this cluster is fact-dense rather than logic-dense, short and frequent review sessions beat long single sittings. A fifteen-minute active-recall pass through group trends every couple of days, tested with a small chapter-mode set at the end of each week, retains far better than a single long revision session the night before a full paper attempt.

### The Organic Chemistry Cluster

Alcohols, Phenols and Ethers, and Aldehydes, Ketones and Carboxylic Acids together form the backbone of Organic Chemistry questions on MHT CET, with reaction-mechanism and reagent-identification questions appearing in nearly every shift. Amines, tested through basicity comparisons and standard preparation methods, and Biomolecules, tested through direct factual recall on carbohydrates, proteins, and nucleic acids, complete the high-weightage organic chapters. Organic Chemistry as a whole rewards students who build a personal reference sheet of named reactions and reagents rather than relying on recognition alone during the exam.

When you drill this cluster in chapter mode, resist the urge to just re-read your notes between attempts. Actively predicting the product of a reaction before checking the answer key, rather than passively recognising it once shown, is what actually builds the pattern-matching speed this cluster demands under exam time pressure.

## Maths: Complete Chapter-by-Chapter Weightage

Maths weightage on MHT CET is concentrated in three clusters: algebra and probability, calculus, and coordinate geometry with vectors.

### The Algebra and Probability Cluster

Probability is the single most consistently weighted chapter across the entire Maths syllabus, with questions on conditional probability, Bayes' theorem, and probability distributions appearing in almost every shift we analysed. Permutations and Combinations, and the algebra of Matrices and Determinants, follow with moderate but steady weightage, and both reward speed and accuracy in routine calculation over conceptual depth.

Given how consistently Probability appears, it is worth treating as close to a guaranteed-marks chapter rather than an optional one. Timed chapter-mode sets, run specifically to build calculation speed rather than to learn new concepts, tend to produce the biggest score gains here once the underlying theory is already solid.

### The Calculus Cluster

Definite Integrals is the heaviest-weighted chapter in the calculus portion of the syllabus, frequently combined with properties of definite integrals and area-under-the-curve applications that test the same underlying skill in slightly different packaging. Differentiation and its applications, particularly maxima-minima and rate-of-change problems, and Differential Equations round out a cluster that, taken together, accounts for a substantial share of the entire Maths section.

Calculus errors are disproportionately careless-mistake errors rather than conceptual gaps, so a useful drilling habit is to solve each problem twice in separate sessions, once for method and once purely for calculation accuracy under a timer, and compare the two. Persistent gaps between your method accuracy and your timed accuracy point directly at where a chapter-mode drill will help most.

### The Coordinate Geometry and Vectors Cluster

Vectors is one of the three highest-weighted chapters in all of Maths, with questions on dot product, cross product, and vector equations of lines and planes appearing consistently. Straight Line and Circle, the two foundational coordinate geometry chapters, are close behind, and their weightage tends to be stable from year to year since the question patterns are among the most standardised on the entire paper.

Because the question patterns in this cluster are so standardised, this is one of the most efficient clusters in the entire syllabus to master through repetition. A student who works through a large chapter-mode set of Vectors, Straight Line, and Circle questions until the standard setups become instantly recognisable will find this cluster consistently produces near-full marks with comparatively little ongoing revision required.

## How to Turn Weightage Data Into a Study Plan

Knowing the weightage list is the easy part. Turning it into a study plan that actually improves your score requires a few deliberate steps.

### Step 1: Audit Your Current Accuracy Per Chapter

Before you can prioritise, you need to know where you currently stand. Take a full paper or a set of chapter-mode tests across every chapter listed above, and record your accuracy per chapter, not just your overall score. A student scoring 90% overall can still be losing most of their available marks in one or two specific high-weightage chapters, and that gap will not show up in an overall percentage.

### Step 2: Build a Weightage-vs-Accuracy Priority Matrix

Once you have both numbers, cross-reference them. Chapters that are high-weightage and low-accuracy are your highest priority: every hour spent there has the largest expected impact on your score. Chapters that are high-weightage and already high-accuracy need maintenance, not new study time; a light revision pass closer to the exam is usually enough. Low-weightage, low-accuracy chapters are the lowest priority in a time-constrained revision schedule, and low-weightage, high-accuracy chapters need essentially no further attention at all.

### Step 3: Drill With Chapter Mode, Not More Full Papers

Once you have identified your highest-priority chapters, the fastest way to close the gap is focused chapter-mode practice, not another full paper. A full paper gives you, at most, two or three questions from any single chapter, which is not enough repetition to fix a specific weakness. A chapter-mode test targeting just Rotational Dynamics, or just p-Block Elements, or just Definite Integrals, gives you the volume of targeted repetition that actually shifts your accuracy number.

### Step 4: Re-Test and Confirm the Improvement

After a focused chapter-mode drilling session, confirm the improvement with a fresh attempt a few days later rather than assuming the work is done. Chapter-accuracy analytics exist precisely so you are not guessing whether your revision worked; check the number, and if it has not moved enough, drill again before moving on to the next priority chapter on your matrix.

## A Sample Six-Week Schedule Using This Data

To make the four steps above concrete, here is how a six-week revision window built around weightage data might look in practice. This is a template to adapt, not a fixed schedule, since your own accuracy audit will move certain chapters up or down the priority order.

Weeks one and two are for the accuracy audit and the highest-priority chapters identified by the matrix: typically Rotational Dynamics and Circular Motion in Physics, Chemical Kinetics and p-Block Elements in Chemistry, and Probability and Vectors in Maths, run as isolated chapter-mode sets followed by a mixed set combining all six. Weeks three and four move to the second tier of high-weightage chapters, Electrostatics, Current Electricity, Electrochemistry, Organic Chemistry's Alcohols and Aldehydes cluster, and Definite Integrals, using the same isolated-then-mixed pattern.

Week five is for full papers under real exam conditions, at least two attempts, each followed immediately by an accuracy review to catch any chapter that has quietly slipped since your original audit. Week six is a maintenance week: light revision passes on every high-weightage chapter, one final full paper roughly three days before the exam, and no new chapters introduced this late regardless of how tempting it feels to cover more ground. Introducing unfamiliar material in the final week almost always costs more in exam-day confidence than it adds in coverage.

## Common Mistakes Students Make With Weightage Data

Weightage data is powerful, but it is also easy to misuse. These are the three mistakes we see most often once students discover it.

### Chasing Weightage While Ignoring Your Own Weak Areas

The priority matrix in the previous section exists because weightage alone is an incomplete signal. A chapter can be high-weightage and already be your strongest chapter, in which case pouring more hours into it produces diminishing returns compared to fixing a weaker chapter with similar weightage. Always cross-reference weightage against your own accuracy data before deciding where to spend the next study session.

### Treating Weightage As Fixed Forever

The chapters listed in this article reflect five years of aggregated data, and that aggregate is stable enough to plan around, but it is not a permanent guarantee for every future shift. Revisit weightage data periodically rather than locking in a study plan in January and never checking whether it still holds, particularly as new past-year papers become available each year and get added to the analysis.

### Studying Breadth Over Depth in High-Weightage Chapters

Skimming five extra chapters at a shallow level is rarely worth more than mastering one high-weightage chapter deeply enough that you can solve any reasonable variation of its standard question types under time pressure. Depth in Rotational Dynamics or Definite Integrals or p-Block Elements produces more marks, more reliably, than shallow familiarity with a wider spread of low-weightage topics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does chapter weightage change every year?

The broad pattern is stable, since it reflects the underlying syllabus emphasis rather than a specific year's paper-setting choices, but small shifts do happen from year to year. That is exactly why this analysis is built from five years of aggregated shifts rather than a single year, and why we recommend revisiting the data periodically rather than treating any single year's paper as the full picture.

### Should I skip low-weightage chapters entirely?

No. Low-weightage does not mean zero-weightage, and a chapter that produces one question a year can still be the difference between two adjacent percentile bands. The right approach is to give low-weightage chapters a lighter pass, enough to answer a standard question correctly if it appears, while reserving your deepest, most repeated practice for the high-weightage chapters covered in this guide.

### How often should I re-check my chapter accuracy?

After every focused drilling session on a specific chapter, and at minimum after every full paper attempt. Chapter accuracy can drift both up and down as you move through the syllabus, and checking it regularly is the only way to know whether your current study plan is actually working rather than just feeling productive.

### Does this weightage data apply to JEE Main too?

Not directly. While MHT CET and JEE Main share a broadly similar Physics, Chemistry, and Maths syllabus, their question-setting patterns and relative chapter emphasis differ, partly because JEE Main draws from a CBSE-aligned syllabus document while MHT CET follows the Maharashtra state board syllabus. Use MHT CET-specific weightage data for MHT CET preparation, and treat any crossover benefit as a bonus rather than a substitute for exam-specific practice.

### I only have four weeks left. How should I use this data differently?

With a short runway, lean much more heavily on the priority matrix from the study plan section and be willing to deprioritise low-weightage chapters almost entirely rather than giving them even a light pass. Spend the majority of your remaining time on chapters that are both high-weightage and currently low-accuracy for you personally, since that is where a few focused days produce the largest realistic score movement. Chapters that are already high-accuracy need only a brief revision pass in the final week to stay sharp, not further deep study.

### Does weightage differ between the PCM and PCB streams?

The Physics and Chemistry weightage patterns described in this article apply to both the PCM and PCB streams, since those two subjects are common to both. Maths weightage, naturally, applies only to PCM aspirants, while PCB aspirants should look for Biology-specific weightage guidance separately once that stream's data is available.

## Putting This Into Practice

Weightage data only produces results when it is paired with focused practice and honest accuracy tracking. Start with whichever subject feels weakest, run a chapter-mode test on its highest-weightage chapter from the lists above, and check your chapter-accuracy report immediately after. That single loop, repeated across your highest-priority chapters over the next few weeks, is the fastest path from knowing this data to actually improving your MHT CET score.
